Requirements

Baseline Rules

  • Aged 18 or over
  • English at level B2 (a UK degree usually satisfies it)
  • £1,270 in personal funds held for 28 days, unless you are applying for permission to stay and have been in the UK with permission for 12 months
  • A new business you founded or are founding, not a role in someone else's
  • The business is your main work, and you run it day to day

"New" binds only people joining a business: your own company can trade before endorsement, and prior trading helps viability. Moving an overseas business in does not carry the innovation by itself.

A skilled side job (RQF level 3 or above) is allowed, but most of your time must go to the business. A job that displaces your day-to-day role can cost the endorsement, and still working half-time elsewhere at the end of the visa reads as a business that is not growing.

The Three Tests

  • Innovative: customers would switch to it, and a typical competitor could not copy it quickly. The Home Office's guidance for endorsing bodies gives adding an app to a taxi or cleaning company as an example that fails.
  • Viable: the plan is realistic for your skills and your money, and there is evidence of real demand.
  • Scalable: there is a structured plan to grow nationally and internationally and to create skilled jobs, so that doubling customers needs far less than double the costs. A licensable method or repeatable team delivery counts; a business that stays in one city, or depends on the founder's own hours, does not.

Assessors weigh the file as a whole at the margin, so strong innovation can offset a thin financial plan.

Self-Check Questions

Innovation

  1. What differs from the alternatives customers use today?
  2. Who buys, and why would they switch?
  3. Could others copy it without new R&D or upskilling?
  4. What do you personally design?

Viability

  1. What research proves demand?
  2. Do your skills fit the business?
  3. Are your aims realistic for your money and skills?
  4. What funding is needed, and who provides it?

Scalability

  1. Is the innovation core to revenue, and when does revenue start?
  2. What evidenced plan takes you national and international?
  3. What is the plan for skilled jobs?

Innovation Paths and Examples

An idea need not be new to the world.

  • Import a model the UK lacks (the test: could a UK consumer already buy it, Amazon-style).
  • Move a model across sectors, if the move needs real applied development.
  • Open to small buyers what only large corporations had.

Re-targeting the same service at a new demographic fails, because any provider could hire for it.

  • Sensor chocolate cup: inventive and copy-proof, but fails because nobody wants it.
  • Crisp machine: fails against established snack brands, passes as a home appliance.
  • Non-alcoholic drink: passes on a novel process alone.
  • Restaurant: passes on customer-influenced design, fails on a different cuisine.
  • Consultancy: passes on extreme specialism and contracts.

Common Refusals

  • Switching cost: the most common innovation refusal is a modest edge over an incumbent customers are locked into. Quantify the buyer's cost of switching, including their new costs (integration, process change, learning), not just your feature gap.
  • Easy to copy: without IP, development complexity, deep market knowledge and a defined proprietary method still count. The yardstick is an ordinary competitor in your sector, not a well-funded one.
  • Route to market: a sales channel or distribution alone does not qualify; it can only be the scaling mechanism.
  • Feature grid: a competitor grid where rivals hold most features answers the innovation question against you. Show indirect and free alternatives and your own weaknesses instead of 2 or 3 hand-picked rivals.
  • Not really UK: the innovation, its control and the head of operations must be in the UK, and the demand must be UK demand; an operating arm of an overseas business fails. Export share does not matter.
  • Other shapes: generic retail and restaurants, another health app or beauty product, basic aggregators, price-based competition and bought franchises. Innovator International stated in 2021 that it also excludes adult, crypto, and political or religious projects; no published list exists.

Scalability in Practice

Plan growth in phases (one city then more on the same playbook, one sector then adjacent ones, direct sales then a channel), with each phase's assumptions visible.

Other Routes

To join an existing business, Skilled Worker is the usual route. Scale-up needs 6 months of sponsored employment, then allows self-employment on a 5-year clock. Global Talent (digital technology) needs no company, but its endorsement letters must come from organizations that have known your work for at least 12 months.

How They Differ

  • Innovator International: allows part-time work alongside the business, checks about 12 months of runway, runs 2 checkpoints, and likely refuses a product that only combines existing components.
  • Envestors: takes no plan upload (you complete its own online form), and payment starts a 4-week clock, so finish the plan before paying. It requires full-time involvement and an in-house technical team member the business already has, checks about 24 months of runway (reported), wants your settlement targets named at endorsement, and runs checkpoints at 12, 24 and 36 months plus a monthly trading report.
  • UK Endorsing Services: treats innovation as a first yes or no gate, scores outsourced development in bands (largely outsourced scores zero), expects around 21% of revenue spent on R&D, and reads very low startup costs as weak innovation. It has reportedly accepted combining existing components since about Oct 2024, and allows part-time work if the business stays the main activity.

Bodies cannot give individual pre-application advice (barred since 2023), so use their free webinars and public Q&A for general questions. A refusal at one body does not count against you at another.

Scams

  • Deal with the bodies directly: scammers forge endorsement letters and falsely claim to partner with a body.
  • Bodies cannot partner with plan writers. A bought plan passes the written stage and fails the interview, and a bought idea is a flat refusal.

Preparing the Plan

Founder Skills

You need sector or technical skill, ideally both, plus the business skill to sell and run the company. Gaps are fine if you name them and budget to fill them.

Own the Technology

You may subcontract the coding, but you must design the innovation yourself and have someone technical own the build. Home Office guidance gives a sole founder of an AI platform with no technical skill as unlikely to meet the innovation test. A no-code or AI-generated build is fine for testing the idea but reads as easily copied when a non-technical founder submits it.

The Business Plan

There is no official template. 30 to 60 pages is normal, shorter can work, and past 60 it gets hard to assess. Write it yourself: the interview tests your own understanding. AI is fine for structure, English, desk research, financial logic checks and a harsh critique of your own plan. It must not generate the core idea, chase trends or supply pasted answers. Show the product with screenshots or a demo.

Most of your income has to come from the innovation you are endorsed for, at endorsement and again at settlement. Consultancy and other services are the usual way founders fail this; if services will fund the build, show their share of income falling over time.

Test fit by working backward from the settlement jobs target (to employ 5 people, what revenue is needed?). The plan need not reach the targets by year 3, only show a credible path.

Write forecasts you can beat: model 3 scenarios, and divide annual sales by 365 to check the daily figure is believable. Forecasts become the benchmark at every checkpoint and at settlement. Write any allowance into this first plan, because it cannot be added later: pre-revenue or loss-making years, a long development phase, or targets that will take 5 to 6 years rather than 3. Pre-revenue, you are judged on the metrics you nominate, so choose them deliberately.

Evidence of Demand

  1. Run at least 10 problem interviews with likely customers (what wastes their time, what costs them money). Interview buyers, not only users, and ask what they already tried and why it failed.
  2. Test a priced solution with them. A survey run after building reads as leading.
  3. Collect letters of intent from the people who hold the budget, sent from company email addresses. Each letter should say why they want it and what they would pay.

Desk research alone is weak, though small qualitative samples are accepted. Aim for 5 to 10 letters; a few signed letters from real buyers beat a survey of hundreds. One can be enough if a large customer commits money up front, while a low-priced product needs many more. Letters from people with no LinkedIn history before the application read as fabricated. Name contacts in the appendix and get their permission to be called, since assessors may phone them.

Funding

You need enough to cover the deepest dip in your cash-flow forecast, plus contingency, and about 12 months of runway to forecast profit, with the time to self-sustainability stated. Observed asks range from about £30,000 to £2 million, and a smaller ask carries no penalty if the plan reaches sustainability. A small sum is credible only if key resources (someone to build, someone to sell, a network or a built MVP) are already in hand, and it must cover finding customers, not only finishing the product; a funded year 1 with an unfunded year 2 is a named refusal.

The money must be cash you already hold, shown on about 6 months of bank statements (a bank letter cannot show how long it has been there); it can sit in a personal account. Property, future salary, a grant not yet awarded and a raise planned after arrival do not count. No outside investor is needed: your own or a family member's money is fine if documented. Shares count if they convert easily to cash, and money held abroad must be transferable to a UK account. Build the main forecast on money you hold and show any raise as a second scenario. The plan must also say how you will live while drawing little or no salary.

Money from someone else needs their bank evidence and a signed letter of terms (an investor also supplies a passport). Disclose the source of funds in 3 parts:

  • How the funds were earned
  • Where they are held and how they were injected
  • Any conditions attached, such as equity or repayment

Innovator International asks only for a profit and loss statement and cashflow, 3 years monthly; a balance sheet is worth adding if you can produce one.

Before You Submit

Getting Endorsed

Submitting

Upload one PDF holding the plan, financials and CV: Innovator International's upload page has slots for plan, financials, funds and extras but none for a CV. Assessors read the CV first, and an inflated past role contaminates the file. Also send your passport, evidence of funds, proof of address (it need not be a UK address) and optional appendices and a 2-minute video, and nothing else unless asked. You pay personally, never a third party.

The file must be complete before assessment starts: material sent after the assessor forms a view may not count.

Waiting and Chasing

Silence during the wait is normal. Bodies give no progress updates and do not expedite. Reasonable points to chase: about 4 weeks (enquiry), 40 days (note, if time is critical), 60 days (urgent note). Innovator International's pack says not to chase before day 60. Cases are investigated only after about day 50. A decision inside a week with no questions is usually a refusal.

Interview and Due Diligence

The interview is usually a recorded video call. It tests that you can explain how the numbers build up, what happens if something goes wrong and how you would cover a shortfall. Due diligence covers your identity, sanctions checks and the source of your funds.

After a Refusal

The usual grounds, in order: not innovative enough, no customer validation, weak development plans, unrealistic projections, viability or scalability doubts, and the founder's capability. Some refusals are flat: no intent to build.

  • Second assessment: Innovator International gives 1 free second assessment and reports that 95% fail. It wins only on a fact the assessor missed, never on repeating or amending the plan: say what was missed and what you hold, attach nothing, and expect about 4 to 5 weeks. The form has one box per criterion, so signpost the evidence you hold in the matching box.
  • Appeal: a separate assessor rereads the original submission only. Appeals succeed occasionally, each time on a plan the assessor misread. At Envestors, new information means a fresh paid application, and the Home Office is told an appeal was made.
  • Reapplying: no limit, but £1,000 each time. Assessors read a later application alongside earlier ones and ask why the strongest version was not sent first, and after 2 refusals they doubt you will ever be accepted. Fix the proposition on the feedback first.
  • No legal remedy: no case law exists on the route and whether a body is reviewable is untested. Plan for 2 to 3 attempts, about £3,600 in fees.

Applying for the Visa

Request the endorsement letter only when the visa application is ready: it is valid for 3 months. Upload the plan and evidence again with the application, because the caseworker reads the file afresh. Your body shares its files with the Home Office, assessor notes and the interview included, so the plan and what you said must agree.

Home Office Questions

Some cases get written questions, not an interview, and the weight varies from 3 questions to a 50-email exchange. Topics include who built the product, proof of funds, skills gaps, pricing against competitors, and proof of the customer conversations the plan claims. Replies are often due in about 10 working days, and the Home Office can ask again for proof of customer interest, so keep letters current.

Switching From Another Visa

Most visa holders, Graduate, Student and Skilled Worker included, can switch inside the UK. Visitors cannot and must apply from outside.

  • Graduate: a Graduate visa applied for from 1 January 2027 lasts 18 months, not 2 years (PhD graduates keep 3 years), so start the plan early. It can be used once and allows self-employment, so the business can start on it; lodge the switch before it expires.
  • Student: you can switch once you complete your course, or during a PhD after at least 24 months of full-time study (caseworker guidance reportedly says 12; the Rules govern). Until then, Student leave bars self-employment and business activity, including taking money, accepting orders or becoming a company director; desk research and customer interviews are fine, and a breach risks refusal of later applications. Since 25 November 2025 you may work self-employed on the business once an Innovator Founder application supported by an endorsement is pending.

If you apply before your current visa expires, your permission to stay (called leave) continues while the Home Office decides, but you cannot make a new application during that time. Apply well before it expires, leaving time for a second endorsement attempt. Do not travel outside the UK, Ireland, the Channel Islands or the Isle of Man while the application is pending: it is treated as withdrawn from the day you leave.

Processing Times

The Home Office aims to decide in 3 weeks from outside the UK and 8 weeks from inside. Paid priority takes 5 working days and super priority the next working day.

In practice, founders report 3 to 6 months from application to decision, priority included (as of Oct 2026): priority buys a place in the queue, not a date. Your endorsing body cannot chase the Home Office.

Rising Refusals After Endorsement

Refusals of in-UK applications by main applicants (switches and extensions on this route and the old Innovator route) rose from 3.2% in 2021 to 31.4% in Q2 2026.

Running the Business

Before You Land

  • Start the business bank account: it is the slowest step, and some providers open accounts from abroad.
  • Expect landlords to want 3 to 6 months' rent upfront with no UK credit history, a call on the same money the funding evidence shows.
  • Relocation and setup usually cost 6 to 12 months against the plan's timeline.

First Steps

Until the business account opens, keep business spend separate from personal spend, because settlement looks for both legs of each payment on business statements.

Identity verification is free online only with a UK passport, UK photocard license, UK residence permit or a biometric overseas passport; check for a passport chip first, and switch route after one failed attempt. Otherwise use a participating Post Office or an authorized accountant or formation agent.

By default the first accounts fall due about 2 years out, after the checkpoints. Shortening the first accounting reference period lets you file early so accounts exist when asked (a Companies Act 2006 mechanism; whether bodies use the accounts is not published).

Checkpoints

Your endorsing body meets you at 12 and 24 months and visits your main trading premises at either one; having none by 24 months puts the endorsement at risk. Attend both: an extension needs at least 2 checkpoint meetings.

  • 12 months: progress should be reasonable for the time; profit is not expected.
  • 24 months: the body also expects commercial progress, such as engaged customers or sales, and reviews the past year rather than future plans.

Expect little contact between checkpoints. Keep your own quarterly record (dated milestones, P&Ls, management accounts) in a data room that serves the checkpoints, an extension and settlement.

Changing the Business Plan

Agree any change to your product, market or forecasts with your endorsing body in writing before you make it: progress is judged against your last endorsed plan, and activity the body never approved may not count at settlement. The test is whether the body can still assess you against that plan:

  • Minor: adding a revenue stream, segment, pricing model or market, or lowering a forecast. Carry on, but file it. Ten or more minor changes can add up to a major one.
  • Major: changing the innovation or the problem you solve, or drifting into consultancy. That is a new assessment (£1,000 plus VAT) on the same visa, allowed only once you show you exhausted reasonable attempts at the original plan; a better opportunity is not a ground.

Lowering a forecast is free; do it before the checkpoint it matters for, so you are judged against the revised plan. Written guides read wider than Innovator International's verbal reading (any change to product, market or forecasts), so notify every revision; Home Office confirmation is pending.

Unapproved activity is discounted rather than counted: £1 million of revenue with £950,000 from unapproved consultancy counts as £50,000. A refused change does not withdraw the endorsement; you revert, apply for a full reassessment, or leave the route.

When Endorsement Is Withdrawn

Your endorsing body can withdraw the endorsement if the business never starts, you never move to the UK, it doubts you are genuine, or you stop keeping in contact. The Home Office then normally cancels your permission with 60 days left. If the body is removed from the list or gives up its status, you normally get 90 days. Either way, you should be allowed to find another body. A withdrawal does not bar a new application; explain who withdrew it, why, and what you changed.

Settlement (ILR) After 3 Years

Start the settlement pack about 6 months before your visa ends, since it takes about 8 weeks to write, and apply for the endorsement at least 2 months before expiry. Bodies report 4 to 6 weeks (Innovator International averaged about 25 days as of Oct 2026, longer for ILR and for slow replies to the assessor's questions), and your ILR application must be in before your leave expires. The body must confirm that the business is active, trading and sustainable for the next 12 months, has made significant achievements against the endorsed plan, is run by you day to day, and meets at least 2 of these 7 targets:

  • £50,000 invested and actively spent on the business
  • Customers at least doubled in 3 years and above the average for similar UK businesses (no reliable benchmark exists)
  • Significant R&D plus a UK intellectual property application (a trademark on branding does not count)
  • £1 million annual gross revenue
  • £500,000 revenue, £100,000 of it from exports
  • 10 full-time jobs for settled workers
  • 5 full-time jobs for settled workers, each paying at least £25,000 a year

Bodies count the two jobs targets as 1 target, not 2. Evidence a third target where you can, in case one is disallowed.

Meeting the Targets

  • £50,000: new money you put in, not reinvested profit; your own savings or a documented loan count, and a relative's money should be documented as a gift or loan to you. It need not arrive as one sum: Innovator International accepts money paid in at any point across the visa, a grant included, as long as the bank statements show it entering the business. Both the money in and the spending must show on the company's business account, and spend booked properly through the business counts even before the visa. Development, contractors, marketing, event travel and equipment pass if you can state the business reason. Tie every spend line to a line of the endorsed plan: Innovator International's guide says spend must further the business, but the letter it signs says it must further the plan assessed at endorsement, and the second is the safe reading. Your own salary does not count; check with your body whether visa and endorsement fees do. Avoid employee salaries as proof, spending solely to reach £50,000, and paying suppliers personally or from an offshore account.
  • Jobs timing: each must last 12 months at an average of 30 hours a week, so to settle at 3 years, hire by about month 22, or by about month 10 if a leaver restarts the clock (unsettled; hire early or over-hire by one).
  • Contracts and payroll: put everyone you count, a co-founder included, on a written employment contract: a director paid through payroll with no contract is an office holder, not an employee. Your own salary counts toward neither jobs target. £25,000 roles suit customer success, operations and support, not engineering, so a senior technical hire is extra cost on top. Register for PAYE before the first payday and hold employer liability insurance of at least £5 million from day one.
  • Pay: the rules require each of the 5 jobs to carry a mean salary of at least £25,000, so each role must reach it on its own. Use employer-only pension contributions on counted jobs, since salary sacrifice lowers gross pay below the line.
  • Cost of the 5-jobs target: about £132,000 to £143,000 a year including employer National Insurance (15%) and pension (3%) (2026 to 2027 rates), in addition to the £50,000.
  • Customers doubled: usually best avoided, because the comparison set is undefined. Where comparable customer numbers do not exist, the guidance accepts credible market share analysis showing an above-average share.
  • Revenue that counts: only income the endorsed company invoices and banks for the endorsed product. Revenue run through another business, or work delivered by hand instead of through the product (even for the same customers), does not count. Invoices should name the product so a third party can map the money to it. VAT registration is due at £90,000 of turnover in a rolling 12 months.
  • Settled workers: British, Irish and British Overseas Territories citizens and people with ILR or EU settled or pre-settled status, tested when each job starts. Graduate, Student and Skilled Worker visa holders do not count. Innovator International also wants permanent roles, not fixed-term ones.
  • R&D: the target needs an IP application, not a grant. Start a dated technical R&D record at the start; the R&D report is the weighty part, not the cheapest "applied for" tick. Where nothing can be patented, the Home Office lets a body accept credible peer-reviewed research outputs plus evidence of commercialization instead (from a reviewer independent of the work).
  • Patents and filings: a patent should be professionally drafted, at roughly £6,000 to £7,000 over 3 to 4 years, and filed well inside year 2; self-drafted patents look uncredible. A UK registered design is the cheapest filing (about £60), registers in 1 to 2 weeks, can be filed late, can cover screen layouts and icons, and is the one filing you can do yourself. A trademark counts only when tied to the R&D outcome.
  • Co-founders: you cannot share a target. If both of you rely on the 5 jobs, the business needs 10; on the £50,000, it needs £100,000 in and spent. Innovator International doubles the revenue targets the same way (£2 million, or £1 million with £200,000 from exports), lets only one founder claim customers doubled, and lets two claim R&D only for two separate innovations.
  • Records: from the first payment, keep a spend ledger tying each payment to a line of the endorsed plan, and the bank's own monthly PDF statements, never screenshots. Minute the funding decision the day it happens and file the share allotment with Companies House within a month, since its filing date is an independent timestamp.

The Sustainability Test

The Rules ask whether the business appears sustainable for the next 12 months, weighing its assets and expected income against current and planned expenses. They set no profit requirement. The test looks forward, so evidence it with accounts, bank statements and a 12-month forecast for the endorsed business itself, with sales broken down by income stream.

Bodies read it more strictly than the Rules. Sales must cover all costs, market-rate salaries included (yours too: you need not draw it, but profit must be able to pay it), with no top-ups: Innovator International says founder capital cannot cover a trading shortfall at settlement, and money put in solely to reach settlement is not counted. A loss-making growth phase passes only if the original plan declared it. Sustainability is judged on the whole business, so a loss-making services arm drags it down, and no revenue at all by year 3 is a refusal.

Supply what the body cannot check itself: a working demo, invoices, bank statements, accounts, employment contracts and contracts for repeat or coming orders. Link each section of the update to its evidence (a shared drive works) rather than sending one bulk portfolio, and for jobs add payroll records and an org chart dated by when each role opened. The settlement assessor has not seen your checkpoint notes, so restate everything against the original plan, and describe your daily tasks rather than a title.

How Progress Is Judged

  • Against your own forecast, by trend: you are not expected to hit it, only to be on the way. For a £1 million year-3 forecast, 25% to 30% is about the low end of acceptable, and 10% is nowhere near.
  • In absolute terms too: £100,000 against £1 million reads as good progress, a few thousand against £250,000 as a fail, so a low forecast is no safe hedge.
  • Offsets: strong hiring or exports can carry a revenue shortfall, but a few targets met do not rescue a missed forecast, and revenue without profit is not maturity.
  • Selling, not just building: run customer engagement and a pipeline beside the build and start selling early; first sales weeks before expiry look suspicious.

The Extension Fallback

If progress is significant but short of the targets, the body can offer a same-business endorsement instead, on the same timing as settlement. The extension lasts 3 years, with checkpoints again; extensions can repeat. Below significant progress, the body refuses settlement and offers no extension, so you would need another visa.

A typical extension scorecard rates 5 factors red, amber or green: the state of the innovation, last year's income against forecast (red under 20%, green above 50%), progress for the time spent, whether the business can sustain itself, and whether it can still scale. Red on any of the last 3 is an immediate refusal; founder money funding the business scores amber, not red. Innovator International uses the same scorecard to decide between extension and ILR: mostly amber points to an extension, mostly green to ILR. An unfinished innovation rules out ILR on its own, while red on income only weakens the case if the business is otherwise mature and profitable and the miss is explained. The extension forecast replaces the original plan as the yardstick, so a padded one raises your own bar, and viability and scalability are reassessed even though innovation is not.

You can apply for ILR at any point from 28 days before the 3 years are complete while your leave lasts, so being 6 months short costs 6 months, not another 3 years. The settlement endorsement checks only the business. If the Home Office converts a failed ILR application into an extension, no extra permission fee is due, but the ILR fee is not refunded.

Before Applying for ILR

Family

Your partner and children under 18 can apply as your dependants. Parents, siblings and adult children have no place on this route. Each pays the visa fee and the Immigration Health Surcharge (£1,035 a year for adults, £776 for children). Unless you have all been in the UK for 12 months, each also needs maintenance funds held for 28 days: £285 for a partner, £315 for the first child and £200 for each further child.

  • Partner: married, in a civil partnership, or together for at least 2 years (living together, or able to show ongoing commitment). A partner can work in any job except as a professional sportsperson or sports coach.
  • Settlement timing: children settle with you, and a partner settles at the same time if you are granted settlement. Otherwise a partner applying alone needs 5 continuous years as your partner. The Life in the UK test is needed unless exempt.

Each dependant pays the ILR fee (£3,226) when they settle. For applications made on or after 26 March 2027, partners and children need B2 English speaking and listening, up from B1.

Rule Changes

Earned Settlement

A Home Office consultation (Nov 2025 to Feb 2026) proposed raising the standard wait for settlement from 5 to 10 years. It says most Innovator Founders should keep an accelerated 3-year route, subject to the mandatory requirements, but its proposed earnings test (above £12,570 a year for 3 to 5 years) names no exemption for them. The changes would apply to everyone who does not yet have ILR. No government response had been published as of Oct 2026. Draw a payroll salary above £12,570 from the first trading month and keep the payslips, and expect any change to take effect with little notice.

The Endorsing-Body Contract

The Home Office contract for endorsing-body services runs to 23 October 2028, and the published notice allows an extension. Checkpoint 2 and the settlement endorsement for a 2027 grant fall after that base term unless it is extended, so the open question is who endorses then. The Migration Advisory Committee is reviewing the talent routes, the endorsing-body model included, and intends to finish in winter 2026.

FAQ

Do I Need £50,000 to Get Endorsed?

No. The fixed £50,000 was removed in 2023; it survives only as one settlement target.

Do I Need a Company or Bank Account Before Applying?

No. A company, trading and a business bank account are only needed once you are on the visa, at the checkpoints, extension and settlement.

Can I Apply Without a Co-Founder?

Yes, sole founders qualify. Co-founders each make their own application and pay their own fees on the same plan; see Meeting the Targets. Apply with fewer and add later, or send the strongest founder first. A later applicant must predate the idea, concept or MVP, so someone who joins after it exists is an employee; a late co-founder risks their own endorsement, not yours. A co-founder can run a UK business from abroad without a visa.

Is There a Minimum Shareholding?

Not in the Rules, but hold a significant stake: below 25% draws questions, and 10% to 20% is doubtful. You must also be a director or member of the company and run it day to day, not act only as an investor. Watch this if you gave equity to an incubator or investor early.

Can I Pay Myself a Salary?

Yes, but your salary counts toward neither the £50,000 target nor the jobs targets; see Meeting the Targets.

What Happens if My Business Fails?

See withdrawal and the extension fallback.

Can My Parents Come?

No. Only a partner and children under 18 qualify (see Family); the adult dependent relative route is near-impossible, with about 4% granted (secondary source).

Can I Spend Time Outside the UK?

Yes. The visa sets no absence limit, but you must move to the UK and run the business here, and ILR allows no more than 180 days outside the UK in any 12 months. Once you hold ILR, it lapses after 2 continuous years away.

Can I Claim Benefits?

No. The visa gives no access to public funds, which covers most benefits and housing help.

When Can I Become a British Citizen?

Usually 12 months after ILR (straight away if you are married to a British citizen), once you have 5 years in the UK with no more than 450 days outside it, and no more than 90 in the last 12. The fee is £1,839 (as of Oct 2026).
